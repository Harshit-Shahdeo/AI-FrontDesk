# Purpose

The Conversation Engine is responsible for managing the conversational context between a customer and AI FrontDesk.

Its purpose is to determine which messages belong to the same conversation, when a conversation should become inactive, how historical conversations are retained, and what conversational context should be provided to the AI.

The Conversation Engine sits between the messaging layer and the AI layer.

---

# Problem Statement

A customer may communicate with a business multiple times over a long period.

Those interactions should not necessarily be treated as one continuous conversation.

For example, a customer may contact the business on Monday to ask about an appointment and then return several days later with an unrelated question.

If the system treats the entire customer history as one permanently active conversation, old conversational context may incorrectly influence new interactions.

This can cause problems such as:

- Stale customer intent being carried into new interactions.
- Previous appointment information influencing a new appointment request.
- Old conversational state being interpreted as current state.
- Increasing AI prompt size as the conversation grows.
- Increasing token consumption.
- Increasing response latency.
- Irrelevant historical information affecting AI responses.

The Conversation Engine therefore needs to create clear boundaries between conversational sessions while preserving historical information.

---

# Goals

The Conversation Engine should:

- Create a conversation when a customer starts a new interaction.
- Associate incoming messages with the correct conversation.
- Keep an active conversation alive while the customer continues interacting.
- Expire a conversation after a defined period of inactivity.
- Preserve expired conversations as historical records.
- Allow a customer to have multiple conversations with the same business over time.
- Prevent old conversational context from automatically becoming part of a new conversation.
- Provide a bounded context window for the AI.
- Keep customer level memory separate from conversation history.

---

# Non Goals

The Conversation Engine is not responsible for:

- Generating AI responses.
- Calling the OpenAI API.
- Sending WhatsApp messages.
- Determining customer intent.
- Booking appointments.
- Managing business hours.
- Managing customer profiles.
- Storing long term customer preferences.

Those responsibilities belong to other modules.

# Core Concepts

The Conversation Engine uses four distinct concepts.

## Customer

A customer represents the person communicating with the business.

A customer may have many conversations with the same business over time.

```text
Customer
    │
    ├── Conversation 1
    ├── Conversation 2
    ├── Conversation 3
    └── Conversation N
```

---

## Conversation

A conversation represents a bounded period of interaction between a customer and a business.

A conversation contains messages that belong to that particular interaction.

A conversation can become expired, but it should never be deleted simply because it has expired.

Historical conversations remain available for auditing, analytics, support, and future features.

---

## Message

A message represents one individual communication.

Messages belong to exactly one conversation.

```text
Conversation
    │
    ├── Message
    ├── Message
    ├── Message
    └── Message
```

Messages may originate from the customer, AI FrontDesk, or the system.

---

## Customer Memory

Customer memory is information that may remain useful across multiple conversations.

Examples include:

```text
Customer name
Preferred appointment time
Preferred service
Other relevant long term preferences
```

Customer memory is separate from conversation history.

An expired conversation does not cause customer memory to disappear.

---

# Conversation Lifecycle

A conversation has two states in the MVP.

```text
ACTIVE
   │
   │ 24 hours of inactivity
   ▼
EXPIRED
```

## ACTIVE

A conversation is active while the customer continues interacting within the configured inactivity window.

For the MVP, the inactivity window is **24 hours**.

Every new customer message resets the expiration window.

For example:

```text
10:00 AM
Customer sends message

expiresAt = next day 10:00 AM
```

If the customer sends another message at:

```text
04:00 PM
```

the expiration becomes:

```text
next day 04:00 PM
```

Therefore, a conversation can remain active for longer than 24 hours if the customer continues interacting.

---

## EXPIRED

A conversation is considered expired after 24 hours without customer activity.

The conversation is not deleted.

It remains available as historical information.

When the customer sends a new message after the previous conversation has expired, AI FrontDesk creates a new conversation.

```text
Previous Conversation
        │
        ▼
     EXPIRED
        │
        │
New customer message
        │
        ▼
New Conversation
        │
        ▼
      ACTIVE
```

---

# Conversation Resolution

When a new incoming message arrives, the system determines whether it belongs to an existing active conversation.

The resolution flow is:

```text
IncomingMessageDto
        │
        ▼
Find active conversation
for customer + business
        │
        ├───────────────┐
        │               │
      Found          Not Found
        │               │
        ▼               ▼
Continue           Create new
conversation       conversation
        │               │
        └───────┬───────┘
                ▼
          Store message
```

A conversation is considered active only when its expiration time has not been reached.

---

# Conversation Expiration Policy

The MVP uses a fixed inactivity period of:

```text
24 hours
```

The expiration time is calculated from the latest customer activity.

Conceptually:

```text
expiresAt = lastCustomerMessageAt + 24 hours
```

The expiration policy should be implemented in a way that allows the value to become configurable in the future.

For example, future versions may allow different businesses to use different conversation windows.

```text
Business A → 24 hours
Business B → 48 hours
Business C → 7 days
```

The MVP will use 24 hours for all businesses.

---

# Historical Context Strategy

A customer may have many historical conversations.

The AI should not receive all historical conversations with every new message.

Doing so would unnecessarily increase:

- Prompt size.
- Token consumption.
- Processing time.
- Response latency.
- Risk of irrelevant context influencing the response.

The system therefore uses a bounded context strategy.

For the MVP, the AI may use:

```text
Current conversation
        +
Previous conversation summary
        +
Previous previous conversation summary
        +
Relevant customer memory
```

This means the normal AI context is limited to the current conversation and summaries of the previous two conversations.

Older conversations remain stored in the database but are not automatically included in the AI prompt.

---

# Context and History Separation

The system intentionally separates:

```text
Current Conversational Context
```

from:

```text
Historical Conversation Data
```

and:

```text
Customer Memory
```

These should not be treated as the same thing.

For example:

```text
Customer
    │
    ├── Customer Memory
    │      ├── Name
    │      └── Preferences
    │
    └── Conversations
           │
           ├── Conversation 1
           │      └── EXPIRED
           │
           ├── Conversation 2
           │      └── EXPIRED
           │
           └── Conversation 3
                  └── ACTIVE
```

The AI primarily uses the active conversation and a limited amount of historical context.

---

# Why Conversations Expire

Conversation expiration prevents stale conversational state from influencing future interactions.

For example:

```text
Conversation 1

Customer:
"I want a haircut tomorrow at 3 PM."

AI:
"Your appointment is booked for 3 PM."
```

The customer leaves the conversation.

Several days later:

```text
Customer:
"I need another appointment."
```

If the original conversation were permanently active, the AI could incorrectly associate the new request with the old appointment.

With conversation expiration:

```text
Conversation 1
    ↓
EXPIRED

New message
    ↓
Conversation 2
    ↓
ACTIVE
```

The new interaction begins with a clean conversational context.

# Important Invariants

The following rules should always hold.

### One message belongs to one conversation

A message cannot exist without belonging to a conversation.

### Expired conversations remain stored

Expiration changes the conversational state. It does not delete history.

### A customer can have many historical conversations

There is no limit on the number of conversations a customer can have over time.

### Only one active conversation should exist for a customer and business

At a given time, a customer should not have multiple active conversations with the same business.

### Customer memory survives conversation expiration

Ending a conversation does not erase information that belongs to the customer.

### Historical conversations are not automatically included in every AI request

Only the configured context window is provided to the AI.

# Design Decisions

The following decisions were made for the MVP.

1. Conversations expire after 24 hours of customer inactivity.

2. Expiration does not delete historical conversations.

3. A new message after expiration creates a new conversation.

4. Customer memory is separate from conversation history.

5. The AI does not receive the customer's entire conversation history.

6. The normal AI context is limited to the current conversation and summaries of the previous two conversations.

7. The expiration period should be implemented as a policy so that it can become configurable later.

8. Conversation resolution happens when a message arrives rather than requiring a background process to continuously mark conversations as expired.

---

# Conclusion

The Conversation Engine provides a boundary between raw messaging events and the AI's conversational context.

It allows AI FrontDesk to maintain useful context without treating a customer's entire history as one continuous conversation.

By separating active conversations, historical conversations, messages, and customer memory, the system can keep AI prompts smaller and more relevant while preserving the customer's complete history for future use.

The MVP uses a 24 hour inactivity window and a maximum context window of three conversations, consisting of the current conversation and summaries of the previous two conversations.

This design gives AI FrontDesk a simple foundation for the MVP while leaving room for more advanced memory and context strategies as the product evolves.
