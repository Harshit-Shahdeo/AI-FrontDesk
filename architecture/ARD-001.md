# ARD-001: Separation of Core Backend and Capability Modules

>

Status: Accepted

Date: 2026-07-30

Context

The AI Front Desk platform is intended to support different types of businesses. Not every business requires the same functionality. For example:

A dental clinic may require appointments, FAQs, and payments.
A consultant may only require FAQs and payments.
A restaurant may require orders instead of appointments.

Embedding business-specific features into the core backend would tightly couple the system to one business domain and make future modifications more difficult.

Decision

The backend will be divided into two major parts:

Core Backend

The core backend will contain infrastructure shared by every business.

Examples include:

Authentication / Identity
Business Management
Customer Management
Conversations
AI Orchestrator
Notification System
Capability Registry

The core backend must remain independent of any specific business workflow.

Capability Modules

Business functionality will be implemented as independent capability modules.

Initial V1 capabilities:

Appointment Management
Knowledge Base (Q&A)
Payments

Future capabilities may include:

Inventory
Orders
Lead Capture
CRM
Analytics

Capabilities should communicate with the core through well-defined interfaces and events instead of tightly coupling themselves to the platform.

Consequences
Advantages
Modular architecture
Easier maintenance
Easier feature additions
Businesses can enable only the capabilities they require
Reduced coupling between infrastructure and business logic
Trade-offs
Slightly more architectural planning upfront
Requires clear interfaces between the core and capability modules
