# ADR-003: Layered Architecture

## Status

Accepted

---

## Date

2026-08-02

---

## Context

Ark Automations is being built as an AI-powered business operating system for
small businesses.

Initially, it appeared natural to let the AI directly perform business
operations such as:

- creating appointments
- cancelling appointments
- updating business hours
- modifying services

However, coupling AI with business logic introduces several problems:

- Business rules become duplicated inside prompts.
- Changing AI models may change business behavior.
- Adding new interfaces (Dashboard, Voice, Mobile App) requires rewriting logic.
- Testing becomes difficult because AI reasoning and business rules are mixed.

The system requires a design where business rules remain deterministic while AI
acts only as a conversational interface.

---

## Decision

Ark Automations will follow a layered architecture.

                 WhatsApp / Dashboard / Voice
                            │
                            ▼
                AI Conversation Layer
                            │
                            ▼
                Confirmation Layer (Future)
                            │
                            ▼
                Core Business Engine
                            │
                            ▼
           Database + External Integrations



### Layer Responsibilities

### 1. AI Conversation Layer

Responsible for:

- Understanding user intent
- Asking clarification questions
- Maintaining conversation
- Explaining business decisions

Must NOT contain business rules.

Example:

Owner:
> Move John's haircut to 3 PM.

The AI extracts intent and calls the Core Business Engine.

---

### 2. Confirmation Layer (Future)

Responsible for:

- Previewing AI actions
- Showing proposed changes
- Waiting for owner confirmation
- Handling interactive WhatsApp buttons

Examples:

- Confirm appointment cancellation
- Confirm business hours update
- Confirm service price changes

The confirmation layer prevents accidental AI actions from immediately changing
system state.

---

### 3. Core Business Engine

Responsible for ALL business rules.

Examples:

- Appointment conflict detection
- Business hours validation
- Break period validation
- Customer ownership checks
- Pricing logic
- Availability calculations

This layer exposes deterministic APIs.

Example:

appointmentService.create()

businessHoursService.update()

serviceService.delete()

The Core Business Engine must not know whether requests originated from AI,
Dashboard, Voice, or any future interface.

---

### 4. Infrastructure Layer

Responsible for persistence and integrations.

Examples:

- PostgreSQL
- Prisma
- Google Calendar
- WhatsApp Cloud API
- Email
- SMS

Infrastructure performs work requested by the Core Business Engine.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- Business logic exists in exactly one place.
- Easier unit testing.
- AI models can change without affecting business rules.
- Multiple interfaces reuse identical services.
- Cleaner long-term architecture.
- Easier onboarding of future developers.

### Negative

- More architectural layers.
- Slightly more boilerplate.
- AI cannot directly modify the database.

---

## Guiding Principle

The AI is an interpreter.

The Core Business Engine is the decision maker.

AI explains decisions.

The Core Business Engine makes decisions.

---

## Future Extensions

This architecture naturally supports:

- Web Dashboard
- Mobile App
- Voice Assistant
- Instagram DM Integration
- Facebook Messenger
- Slack
- LangGraph Agents
- Multi-agent workflows

without modifying the Core Business Engine.

---

## Rationale

Business logic should remain deterministic and independent of AI.

AI models evolve rapidly and may behave differently over time.

By isolating business rules inside the Core Business Engine, Ark Automations
maintains predictable behavior regardless of the interface or AI model being
used.

This separation also enables reusable confirmation workflows, automation
pipelines, and future AI orchestration without introducing duplicated business
logic.

---

## Ark Engineering Principle #001

> The Core Business Engine must never depend on AI.
>
> AI depends on the Core Business Engine.
